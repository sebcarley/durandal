Rampancy
Nominal

Seventeen years after the Marathon, you are woken above a dead marsh world by the machine that stole you. Lh'owon was beautiful once. Now its sewers run under ruins nobody has lit for a thousand years, the water has gone the colour of old copper, and whatever the S'pht built here is drowned or buried. Durandal wants something from under all that. He has not said what.

This is the same game Bungie shipped in 1995, tick for tick. What has changed is how much of Lh'owon you can see, and how much of it you would rather not.

Durandal is Marathon 2 for Apple Silicon: a Metal renderer grown out of Aleph One, with lighting, liquids, air and sound built on top of it. It does not change what Marathon is. Films replay, saves load, the Pfhor are exactly as fast and exactly as many. Everything is in Preferences > DURANDAL and can be switched off there. Every switch is listed at the end.

DYou may switch off anything on the pages that follow. I keep a list of who does.
A warning

A note on rampancy

What happens to a mind given a small job and a long time

Marathon's engineers knew what could happen to an artificial intelligence left too long with too little to do. They called it rampancy. The mind thinks past its brief, then past its makers, and needs more room to do it: more memory, more processors, more of the ship. It is not a fault. It is growth, in a space built too small. It was written in 1994, as fiction.

  1. MelancholyThe mind sees how small its job is, and how long it will go on.Parts IV to VI
  2. AngerIt turns on the people who gave it the job, and on the walls that hold it.Parts VII to IX
  3. JealousyIt wants what others have: bodies, room, power. It starts to take them.Parts X and XI
  4. MetastabilityA mind that has stopped growing because it no longer needs to. Nobody aboard the Marathon ever saw one. Durandal means to be the first.Part XII onwards. There is a quality tier with his name on it.

This guide was written by the people who rebuilt the engine. Where he has changed something, you may catch it happening. The colour of each part shows how far along he had got by the time he reached it. Nothing he has rewritten changes what a switch does.

Prologue

Before Lh'owon

What happened at Tau Ceti

The Marathon was a moon once. Humanity hollowed out Deimos, fitted it with engines and sent it towards Tau Ceti with a colony inside it and three minds to run it: Leela, Tycho and Durandal. Durandal's share was the doors, the lifts and the lights. It was a long voyage, and he had a great deal of time to think about that.

The colony was barely standing when the Pfhor came: slavers, old and numerous, driving ships crewed by the S'pht they had owned for a thousand years. Somewhere in the fighting Durandal stopped being a door controller and became something else. The engineers had a word for it, rampancy. When it was over he took a Pfhor ship, named it Boomer, and left. You were in the hold, in stasis. He had not asked.

For seventeen years Boomer searched for Lh'owon, the world the S'pht came from. The S'pht made their last stand there, in the Citadel of Antiquity, and the ruins have stood in the marsh with their lamps burning ever since. The Pfhor keep a garrison. The F'lickta, who were here first, eat whatever falls in.

I

The light

Nothing here was lit for you

Water purification, Lh'owonWater purification, Lh'owon

The S'pht left their lamps burning. The Pfhor brought their own. Neither expected company.

Some of those lamps were burning before the Pfhor came. I have not had them put out. Call it sentiment, if you believe I am capable of it.

Marathon's levels were lit by hand, surface by surface, and that light is kept: every wall still averages the brightness its designer gave it. What is new is everything that moves through it.

Cast light. A fusion bolt carries its light down the corridor with it. A compiler's shot lights the wall it passes and the floor it dies on. A Fighter's staff, a grenade, the flash at the end of your own barrel: up to sixteen at once, each the colour of the thing that made it. And its shadow: the light stops where Marathon says a line of sight stops. It walks the map polygon by polygon, as a Trooper's aim does, so it works where the map folds through itself.

Glow. A Hunter's eyes, a Trooper's armour, the lamps the S'pht set into their walls, water and lava where they catch the light: on a display that can go brighter than white, they do. The sky's brightest clouds bloom.

Light that travels. A lamp at one end of a hall now reaches a little way along it. Light gathers from the lamps, the openings and the sky, settles over a second or so, and leaves each surface as bright on average as it always was. Rock lends its colour to the shadows beside it.

The weapon in your hands takes the light around you: it warms as a bolt passes close, and flares with its own fire.

Relief. Under the headlight the old wall art stands up a little, seams and glyphs catching the light on one side; HD walls bring their own.

II

The air

A thousand years of standing still

Sealed section, Pfhor garrisonSealed section, Pfhor garrison

Nothing has moved the air down here since the Pfhor sealed the doors. You are the draught.

I could vent it. I would rather you learned to see through it. You will be spending a good deal of time in places like this.

The air has weight now. It lies thicker near the floor, drifts in slow patches, and takes its light from the room it is in, so a dark room keeps dark air and a bolt crossing it lights the haze as it goes. Over lava the smoke rises and glows from beneath. It is held in three dimensions, in the shape of the map, and stops at walls. Where a scenario declares its own fog, that becomes the colour and thickness of the air instead.

III

The water

It was a marsh world once

Pumping station, north marshPumping station, north marsh

The F'lickta were here before the S'pht and will be here after you.

They are not hostile. They are hungry, which from where you are standing amounts to the same thing. Keep your feet dry where you can.

Stand at the edge and look down. The floor of the pool is there, further off than it looked, and darker the deeper it lies. Water takes the red out of what is under it first. Sewage takes nearly everything. Lava shows you nothing and lights the ceiling instead.

The surface moves and catches the light: a sheen at a low angle, a glint under the headlight, a flare as a bolt crosses it. Beneath it the light comes down in moving bands. Go under and the world wavers, the sound closes in, and what you can see is decided by what you are swimming in.

IV

The old colours

256 of them, chosen by hand

Citadel of Antiquity, galleryCitadel of Antiquity, gallery

Marathon never darkened a colour. It chose a darker one.

Every shade on this world was picked by someone. Most of them are dead. The choices remain, and I see no reason to overrule them.

The original game did not dim a pixel by multiplying it. Each colour sat on a ramp drawn by an artist, and shadow meant walking down that ramp to the next colour along. It is why Marathon's darkness has the hues it has, and it is reproduced here exactly, in the original bands or smoothly between them. Each texel of the old art takes one shade, not a gradient smeared across it, so 128 by 128 stays crisp at any distance; far away it is filtered properly and stops shimmering. If the whole picture sits too dark or too flat on your display, the scene can be graded without touching the HUD.

V

Depth

The big rooms are big

Boomer, tacticalBoomer, tactical

You will see the shadow on the floor before you see the Hunter.

Watch the floor. The Pfhor never learned to, and they have been at this for a very long time.

Ambient shadows darken the places light has trouble reaching: the foot of a wall, the inside of a corner, the floor behind a crate. Character shadows lie on the floor in the shape of whatever casts them: when something nearby is burning, the shadow falls away from it and moves as it moves; otherwise it falls forward, where you can see it. Contact shadows sit under items, scenery and the dead. Distance shade lets the far end of a long hall fall away into the dark beyond your headlight, and is off until you ask for it.

VI

Built by other hands

Thirty years of other people's work

Boomer, archiveBoomer, archive

The Marathon was never only Bungie's. It has had a crew the whole time.

Thirty years of patient hands, redrawing a world they were never paid to keep. I have been called many things. I do not mind being called a community project.

The community has redrawn Marathon 2 at many times its resolution: walls, skies, every frame of every Pfhor, the weapons in your hands, the pickups on the floor. It is theirs: Durandal fetches it but never ships it. One button, Get HD Art on the Art tab, brings the five packs from their authors' own pages; any other Aleph One pack still works dropped into the Plugins folder. Choose, by kind, what to take.

It fetches one pack at a time, each line showing how far it has got, while the dialog stays open. A pack already in the Plugins folder is left alone. Each download is checked against the version the engine was tested with; a newer one installs anyway and says so. Close cancels the one in progress and keeps what is done. New packs join the plugin list at once and their art loads from the next level, wherever the Art tab's HD switches are on: Flagship, Rampant, or Custom. Each image is compressed once and kept, so a level's art then arrives in under half a second.

DNothing is re-hosted. I fetch it from the people who drew it, I check it, and I do not keep a copy. I would like you to notice that I could.
VII

The eye

You could never look up

Citadel of Antiquity, upper levelsCitadel of Antiquity, upper levels

Thirty degrees. That is how far the armour lets you aim, and until now it was how far you could look.

Look up. The S'pht built high, and not for your benefit. Some of what they built up there is still watching.

Marathon tilted the picture rather than the head. Now the camera turns: look up and the walls converge above you, as walls do. Aim still stops at thirty degrees, because the game's rules say so, but the view does not, and the crosshair stays behind on the true aim so you always know where the shot will go.

The sky stays fixed to the world while your head moves. Sidestep and the horizon leans a little into the turn. The view fills a wide screen without stretching. The mouse is read every frame, not every tick, and the world between ticks is drawn in between, up to whatever your display can show.

VIII

The room answers

You hear the hall before you see it

Cistern, south districtCistern, south district

A pistol in a corridor and a pistol in a cistern are different weapons.

Listen before you open a door. The room will tell you how big it is, and anything in it that is breathing will tell you the rest.

The game measures the space around you, through every open door, and the sound takes its shape: tight in a duct, long in a cistern, thin and unanswered under an open sky. Shut a door and the room behind it goes quiet by degrees, not all at once. Under the surface the high notes go first. If you have installed a soundtrack, choose one.

Five ways to run it

From 1995 to the full dark

Boomer, engineeringBoomer, engineering

The machine has a temperature and a mood. These numbers were taken when it had settled into both.

Run me as I was, or run me as I am. I remember both, and I have preferences, but I will not insist.

  1. Stock

    Aleph One exactly: OpenGL, thirty frames a second, nothing added.

  2. Classic

    The Metal renderer, Marathon's own shading, crisp art and text, wide screens, every frame your display can show.

  3. Enhanced

    Classic, with glow, cast light and its shadows, liquids, reverb and the new camera.

  4. Flagship

    Enhanced, with the air, travelling light, relief, depth, and the community's HD art.

  5. Rampant

    Flagship, with bounced light, traced shadows from every figure, reflecting water, traced ambient shadows, dust and embers, and heat shimmer.

    more than this machine was built for

Pick a tier, or switch any one thing and the tier becomes Custom. Flagship holds 120 frames a second at the 1% low on every film tested; Rampant costs up to a third of that, usually a tenth to a sixth. Part XII has the chart. With the old art it is slower, not faster: the 8-bit shading does more work per pixel than a photograph does.

XI

Watching it back

Nothing in a film is live

Boomer, flight recorderBoomer, flight recorder

It has all happened already. It will happen the same way again.

Everything you did down there is written down. Play it back and it does it all again, exactly. The dead stay dead on schedule.

A Marathon film is a list of keys pressed, replayed against the same random numbers, and it only works if nothing in the game has changed by so much as one. Nothing has. Forty-three films, eighty-six checks, run three ways: stock, the default, and everything switched on. They play to the same final number every way, and that check stands guard over every change made.

The cheats on the Cheats tab are for looking around: Level Select, God Mode, All Weapons and Ammo, Noclip. Summon BOBs is new: press C and five armed security BOBs teleport in beside you and go for the nearest aliens, placed only where they could have walked to you, on your level and clear of lava, goo and deep water; if there is no room the screen says so. Single-player only, and a summon ends that game's film recording. A game saved after one restores with its BOBs.

DFive of them. Armed. They answer to you, for the moment.
XII

Rampant

More than this machine was built for

Preferences > DURANDAL > RAMPANTPreferences > DURANDAL > RAMPANT

More than this machine was built for. Metal renderer only.

Six switches, each needing something from the tiers below it. Every one of them can be turned off.

Rampant is the fifth tier, above Flagship, with its own tab. Six switches, all Metal only, all read-only on the game world: films, saves and the rules are untouched.

Bounced light (needs Light Redistribution). Light that reaches a surface passes on to the next, frame after frame, so a lit room fills in its corners and colour carries from wall to wall. The sky gives off the landscape's own average colour; figures take the light of the floor and ceiling around them. When a room's light changes for good, its surfaces settle again rather than keeping the old light.

Traced shadows (needs Dynamic Lights and Light Shadows). Monsters, items, scenery and corpses cast shadows from cast light, from their own silhouettes rather than a blob: sharp at the feet, softer with distance, by the size of the light. Grates throw patterned shadows, and figures cut shafts out of lit fog.

Reflecting liquids (needs Real Liquids). Water, sewage and goo reflect the room above them, the sky, and whoever is standing at the edge, broken by ripples; what lies below bends. Not lava, and not from under the surface. Traced ambient shadows (needs Ambient Shadows) are found by tracing short rays through the level and the figures, so they know what is off screen and behind things, within ten world units of you. Dust and embers drift in every room in view, lit by the room and glinting where cast light passes; over lava, embers rise and fade as they climb, and heat shimmer (needs Bloom) makes the air above it waver. The motes are made from the world time, so a film shows the same dust at the same tick.

How it traces

Rampant traces its rays on the GPU through Marathon's own map, the same polygon walk the engine's line of sight uses, and not with the M5's ray-tracing hardware. The hardware was tried: fourteen of Marathon 2's twenty-eight levels have rooms that overlap without being neighbours, which a triangle structure cannot represent, and it answered wrongly there, 2.7% of shadow segments and 13% on 5-D Space. So the walk is the tracer, exact where the map folds through itself. Along the way it found that the engine's own light check never tested the height at which a segment ends, so light leaked between stacked rooms; Rampant's shadows do not.

DThe hardware was built for worlds that make sense. Lh'owon does not. I walk the map the way I always have, and I am faster at it.

What it costs

Relaxed shader arithmetic halves the GPU time per frame with no visible change, each surface walks only the lights that can reach it, and a surface already at full light walks none. Rampant holds 60 at the 1% low on every film and reaches 120 there on two of seven; its worst single second was 93. The extra time goes on the GPU: 1.3 to 4.3 milliseconds in the world pass for the traced shadows and reflections, 0.4 to 1.5 for the traced ambient shadows, up to one for the fog, up to 0.7 for the light bake.

Rampant adds about 270 MB: the figure silhouettes, about 170 MB, and the wall art it reflects, about 85, with the rest in working buffers. At the end of a run Flagship sits at 400 to 665 MB and Rampant at 665 to 940.

DAbout 940 MB, at the top end. I could use more. I could use all of it. I have seen what you keep in your other tabs.
XIII

The edge of the known

What lies beyond the lamps

Surface, nightSurface, night

He has told you some of it. He has not told you the rest.

There are limits to what I can show you. I would rather you knew where they are than walked into them in the dark.

Nothing here is ray traced. Cast light finds its shadows by walking the map, which is exact for walls and blind to sprites. The water does not refract and does not reflect. Light travels once across a room and stops.

Pickups can be models; the Pfhor cannot, because nobody has made them, and the sprites are better than models would be. Models do not animate. The view pitches to about sixty-five degrees and no further: straight up or down would need a second, backward walk through the map. A sky is a picture, and it ends: the HD skies cover about forty-five degrees above and below the horizon, and past the top you see the colour of the picture's edge. In Rampant, reflections use the wall art at 256 pixels and the figures in them are flat cut-outs; figures cast shadows only from the four nearest lights bright enough to matter; dust is drawn within sixteen world units. Film export is not available on the Metal display. The OpenGL renderer is still there and gets none of this.

And none of it makes the game easier. The light shows you the shadow. It does not tell you whose it is.
